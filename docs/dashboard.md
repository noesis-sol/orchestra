# Dashboard

What the terminal shows while a run goes and after it ends, its keys, the notifications, and the answer talk beside it. Back to the [README](../README.md).

## What you see

One dashboard, updated in place on the terminal's alternate screen, as a full-screen program such as `less` draws: nothing is printed above it while the loop runs, and resizing the pane leaves no earlier frames behind.

While the run winds down (**s**, then **y**), the foot reads:

```
 ■ ■ ■ ■  Winding down · 3 running, no new tickets
  1–3 to go to a worker's tab · s to keep taking tickets
```

- **Load**, on a line of its own above the totals and set apart from the title by a blank line, says whether the workers are overloading the machine: the workers at work out of how many may (a ticket merging, waiting to merge or handed back to its worker has freed its slot, and isn't counted, though Current shows it); the memory their process trees take (each worker's claude and everything under it: its tests, lint and builds), and orchestra's own commands', its merge check above all, from one process listing (`ps`) every 5 seconds, apart in a pane wide enough: `memory 4.2 GB (workers 2.8, merge check 1.4)`; the kernel's memory pressure (`normal`, `warn` or `critical`: on macOS `kern.memorystatus_vm_pressure_level`, on Linux `/proc/pressure/memory`, or without it the share of `MemAvailable`, which follows in brackets: `pressure normal (6.0 GB available)`), and `swapping 1200 pages/min` while pages are swapped in and out at 600 a minute or more (`vm_stat`, `/proc/vmstat`); and the 1-minute load average against the cores, `CPU 9.8/10 cores`. Memory used against total says little on macOS, whose spare memory holds cache and compressed pages: the pressure and the swapping say whether memory is short. Each figure takes the colour of its own signal, so the colour says which one is pressed: the CPU is green, amber once the load passes 1.5 times the cores, red once it has stayed above 3 times the cores for a minute; the pressure green at `normal`, red at `warn` or worse; the swapping, shown only while under way, amber; the memory, which has no threshold, uncoloured (the thresholds are in `internal/load`). Each worker's tree starts at the process its terminal names for its pane (Herdr's `pane process-info`, tmux's `pane_pid`, the process orchestra launched in a cmux terminal); a worker whose tree can't be found is counted apart, `(…, 1 unknown)`, and reads `? GB` in Current, not 0. A narrow pane drops the memory's breakdown, then the memory; a pane a line too short for the blank line above it has none, losing nothing else for it, and one too short for the strip and this line has the workers on the totals' one line (`▶ 2/3`). The line mostly only shows: the one thing the run does with it is leave the time the pressure or the CPU is red, or swapping under way, out of a [hand-back's time limit](running.md#several-tickets-at-once).
- **Totals** for the run, as one strip: completed, set aside (deferred or left for review, and how many triaged), what needs you (questions to answer, and finished tickets blocked from merging), and the ready tickets waiting for a slot out of the tickets not closed (`9 of 16`: open, in progress, blocked or deferred; in a run scoped to a ticket, the scope's), the second number following tickets as they close. **Set aside** counts the tickets whose rows read `↷ deferred` or `! review`, and only while they do: one the run takes back (reopened by a hold for the [environment](running.md#several-tickets-at-once), merged once its check runs again, or merged once its worker, left in its tab, closed it there: [`LATE_CLOSE`](running.md#tickets-set-aside)) no longer counts, and one that then merges counts only under **Completed**. A ticket blocked from merging (`■ blocked`) counts under **Needs you** instead; the run's closing notification counts it set aside with the rest. **In queue** reads `—` until the loop first says, and `· held` follows it, in amber, while the run winds down. Ready tickets that wait for something besides a free slot don't count among those waiting for one: `· 2 held back` follows the count, in yellow, while some do, whether their footprint overlaps a running ticket's, their subtickets are not all closed and merged, a ticket blocking them (or the one they were filed from) has not merged, or a solo ticket runs or is next. Those whose worktree's setup failed ([`SETUP_FAILED`](running.md#several-tickets-at-once)) follow apart, also in yellow, as they need the setup fixed rather than another ticket: `· 1 setup failed`. Held back is per ticket, `held` the whole queue; the log says why each one waits (`skipping …: touches …, like running …`, `… waits: …`). A pane too narrow for the strip gets the totals on one line, `queue 9 of 16 · 2 held back` among them. The branch and how long the run has gone are on the title line, with the run's scope between them (`batch/2026-09-28 · ticket kinieta-kco · 18m40s`; `· feature <epic>` for a feature, `· spec <path>` for a [spec](running.md#a-whole-app-from-a-spec)). While the run holds for the account's [usage limit](running.md#the-usage-limit), starting no new tickets, the title line says so after the version, in yellow, with when the limit resets: `usage limit · resets 14:05`, the day added when it isn't today (`resets Tue Oct 20 14:05`), or `usage limit` alone when Claude Code didn't say. It goes once the hold lifts. An [overnight](running.md#overnight) run has a small blue `overnight` badge after the version for the whole run; a pane too narrow for the whole title line drops the badge before cutting anything else.
- **Tickets**: under a bold `Tickets` label, indented like `Current` but in a neutral colour of its own (cyan is for live work, green, yellow and red for the tickets' states), one row per ticket, updated as it moves, in the order the tickets came to an end. Beside Current the table is the run's record of the tickets that came to an end (done, set aside, stopped, waiting, waiting on you): it leaves out the tickets Current shows, and a ticket's row comes in, under the others, as it leaves Current. Where Current isn't drawn (a pane too short for it, the run's summary), the running tickets keep their rows, so a running ticket is always shown somewhere. A **picked-up** ticket (cyan) shows its title. Once its worker has finished and orchestra has taken its work, a ticket waiting for its turn behind another ticket's merge reads `◼ to merge`, in blue, and one whose merge is under way (the rebase onto the moved branch, if it moved, the check, the fast-forward) `◆ merging`, in pink: merges go one at a time, in the order the tickets finish, and the check can take minutes. Neither counts as completed yet. One handed back to its worker meanwhile reads `⟳ resolving` (its rebase's conflicts) or `⟳ fixing` (its check failed before it merged), in yellow, then `◼ to merge` or `◆ merging` again. A **completed** one (green) shows only the merged commit, or `no change to merge` for one closed with nothing of its own to merge. A **deferred** one (yellow) shows why, replaced by the triage organ's verdict (purple `◆`) once it's in. One left for review (`CHECKS_FAILED`, closed without a commit, …) reads `! review` and says why in a few words (`checks failed`, `closed without a commit`); the log has the rest. One whose work is done but which can't merge until you act reads `■ blocked`, in red, as a worker's box says `blocked — waiting for you`, and says why: `main checkout has uncommitted changes` or `main checkout is not on <branch>` (`DIRTY_TREE`), `merge conflict in <files>` (`MERGE_CONFLICT`), `git failed` (`GIT_FAILED`), `does not fast-forward onto <branch>` (`MERGE_FAILED`); it counts under **Needs you**. A ticket that started before a ticket blocking it merged, and is back in the queue until it does, reads `◷ waiting` and `for <blocker> to merge`, dimmed, until it is picked up again; one set aside for failing at once and reopened by a hold for the environment reads `◷ waiting` and `back in the queue: the run held for the environment`. A ticket whose worker was still running when the run stopped, or that stopped the run otherwise (`PAUSED`, say), reads `■ stopped`, in red. The table, its label included, takes about a third of the pane's height, room for 3 to 10 tickets, and keeps its place and height while workers start, report and finish: blank rows fill it until it has that many rows to show, after which the oldest give way to a `+N earlier tickets, see the log` row. Only a resize changes it, and a pane too short for the table and a line for each worker the run may have (`-c`) leaves it out. The run's summary lists every ticket.
- **Current**: a box per running ticket, with its number (the key that goes to its tab, below), the worker's status, elapsed time, the memory its process tree takes (as the load line counts it, to spot the heavy one), the ticket title and the worker's latest action, its line blank until the worker first reports. The boxes are all as tall as the tallest, so they keep their height as workers report. Below the tickets table, when what is left of the pane can't hold a box each, with room for the line that says the run winds down, the workers take one line each in a single box; that changes only as workers start and finish, or on a resize. In a pane 120 columns wide or wider the boxes go two to a row, numbered row by row (`1 2` over `3 4`), the two in a row equally tall; an odd one out keeps the left column's width. A narrower pane stacks them, and a resize takes effect on the next frame. The worker's status reads cyan while it works (`working`, or what it reports doing: `editing`, `reading`, `subagent`), bold yellow while it runs checks (`testing`), red when it waits for you (`blocked — waiting for you`, `permission — waiting for you`), plain green once it has finished its turn (`done`), blue once orchestra has taken its work and it waits behind another ticket's merge, with its place in line (`to merge (2nd)`, 1st being next), pink while its merge is under way (`merging`; the ticket's row turns bold green when it merges), and yellow otherwise, such as `idle`, `limited` (its turn ended on the account's usage limit), `unknown`, `resolving` or `fixing check`. The spinner before the ticket's ID turns while its worker works, a hand-back included, and stops once it has finished: a static mark takes its place, `◼` while it is to merge, `◆` while merging, `•` otherwise, so motion means a worker at work. A ticket to merge or merging has, for its latest action, what its merge waits for or does: `waiting for its turn to merge into <base>`, `merging into <base> once checked, rebased onto it first if it moved on`. One [checked ahead of its turn](running.md#several-tickets-at-once), as the ticket before it is, says how that goes instead while it waits: `rebasing onto wt/<id>, to be checked ahead of its turn on top of it as it is checked`, `checked ahead of its turn, on top of wt/<id> as it is checked`, then `passed its check ahead of its turn, on top of wt/<id>: merges with no further check if wt/<id> merges as checked` or `failed its check ahead of its turn, on top of wt/<id>: checked again in its turn`; one whose check ahead was stopped, or that wasn't rebased onto the branch ahead, waits for its turn as any other. Each box's border is cyan while its worker runs and red when it's blocked or, as its hooks say, waits on a permission prompt (`permission — waiting for you`, `■ allow?` in the tickets table; see [the hooks](workers.md#worker-prompt)); with several workers, each title takes at most two lines. A ticket set aside with its worker left in its tab, which closes it there, has a box again while it merges ([`LATE_CLOSE`](running.md#tickets-set-aside)), as one finished does. A ticket set aside for a failed check has a box again while its check runs once more after the base moved on, its latest action reading `checking again: <base> moved on since its check failed`, and its status `to merge` or `merging` as it takes its turn; if it then merges, its row turns completed. With no worker running, a single grey box says `picking the next ticket…` (or `stopping…`). It updates every 3 seconds. In the smallest panes the `Current` label is the first thing left out, and the `Tickets` label goes with it.

- **Keys**: the hint along the bottom names each key and what it does, the key brighter than its words: `1–3 to go to a worker's tab · s to stop after the current tickets`, or in a narrow pane `1–3 worker tab · s stop after current`, then `s` alone. **1** to **9** switch to that worker's tab, in Herdr with `herdr tab focus`, in tmux by selecting its window (`tmux select-window`), in cmux by selecting its workspace (`cmux select-workspace`): the running workers are numbered in Current, oldest first, up to the ninth, and when one finishes those after it move up. The hint names the numbers there are. They do nothing while the stop question is open; if the terminal can't switch, the log says why and the dashboard carries on. In tmux they work only when the workers' windows are in the session `orchestra` runs in ([Herdr, tmux or cmux](running.md#herdr-tmux-or-cmux)): run outside tmux, or with `--workspace` naming another session, the hint leaves the numbers out, and a line above it says how to see the windows instead, ` to see the workers' tabs: tmux attach -t orchestra-kinieta`. **s** asks, in a box over the dashboard (a line above the hint in a small pane), whether to stop after the running tickets: `Stop after the running tickets? Stopping after the 2 running tickets finish (kinieta-kco, kinieta-y6j): no new tickets will start. They merge as usual, then the run ends.` **y** confirms, **n** or **Esc** closes it; the question names y and n as the hint names its keys. The run then starts no new ticket, from any path; the running ones carry on as usual, merges, rebases and re-checks included, and when the last one returns the run ends normally (`Stopped after the running tickets, as asked` on screen, `DRAINED after 12 tickets` in the log, exit code 0), with triage and the report. The log says `DRAIN: stopping after the 2 running tickets finish (…): no new tickets will start, asked from the dashboard`, and the report's first sentence mentions it. With nothing running, it ends at once. Meanwhile a line above the hint shows how far the wind-down has come, without naming the tickets (Current and the tickets table do): a `■` for each ticket running when it began, in amber while it runs, green once it merges, red once it is set aside or waits on you, then `Winding down` in bold amber and how many still run, `■ ■ ■ ■  Winding down · 3 running, no new tickets` once the first has merged, or `nothing running, ending now`. Amber means only this: winding down is your choice, and nothing needs you. The line never wraps: a narrower pane drops `, no new tickets`, then the count, then keeps the squares alone. The totals mark the queue `held`, in amber; and the hint reads `s to keep taking tickets`: **s** offers `Keep taking tickets?` to take it back (logged as `DRAIN cancelled`). A run stopping for another reason (see [Several tickets at once](running.md#several-tickets-at-once)) has no **s** to offer: the hint names only the number keys, and nothing once no worker runs. **Ctrl+C**, which the hint leaves out, stops at once at any time, the question open or not, leaving the workers running; the run then waits for a merge already under way, and a second Ctrl+C quits without waiting (see [Run](running.md#run)). Outside the dashboard (`--plain`, scripts), `kill -USR1 <pid>` asks the same without a question. **a** opens the [answer talk](#the-answer-talk) while questions wait, or goes to its pane once it is open; the hint names it then (`a to answer 2 questions`, `a question for you · a to answer`).

When the loop stops, the dashboard closes and the terminal shows what it showed before the run, followed by the run's summary (the title, the totals and every ticket of the run, however many there are), its closing line and the run report (see [Organs](organs.md#organs)). A run that ended by itself closes in bold green with how it ended, then, quieter, how many tickets and how long it took: `♪ Completed the Run  12 tickets · 1h12m` when the queue is empty, `♪ Reached the ticket limit (40)`, or `♪ Stopped after the running tickets, as asked` after **s**. A run that ended with nothing ready but questions you left open in the answer talk names them, and the tickets each holds, on a line of its own in yellow: `QUESTIONS_OPEN: <question> (holding <ids>): answer with orchestra answer, then run again`. With tickets still waiting on a setup that failed in their worktrees, a line of its own in yellow names them, with where each one's output is: `SETUP_WAITS: <id> waits on the setup that failed in its worktree (…); fix the setup, then run again`. A run scoped to a ticket adds its scope on a line of its own: `SCOPE_DONE: …` in green, or `SCOPE_OPEN: …` in yellow, as work is left. A run something stopped ends with its red line instead, saying why (`■ PAUSED: …`). The organ phase's lines follow in light purple after a `◆`. While triage finishes and while the run report is written, a spinner takes the `◆`'s place, with the time so far: `⣾ Writing the run report with Claude… 0:42 (Ctrl+C skips)`. When the step ends, its line becomes its outcome and how long it took: `◆ Triage finished (12s)`, `◆ Run report written (1m04s)`, `◆ Run report skipped` after Ctrl+C, or the `REVIEW_FAILED` warning. A step over at once, such as triage with nothing queued, shows only its outcome. `Report saved to …` follows the report. The log, the event stream and plain output keep the loop's own words (`READY_EMPTY after 12 tickets`, `finishing triage…`).

A run with [nothing to run](running.md#nothing-to-run) doesn't open the dashboard: nothing ready, or only tickets the run would hold back, and no worker the last run left to carry on with. It leaves the screen as it is and shows a box in its place, then exits 0, without triage, a report or a notification. `✓ All done`, in green, when no ticket is left but epics, which never run, and none is closed but not merged; `Still open: <ids> (bd close <ids>)` follows while epics are open. A spec run whose root epic is all done says `✓ <path> is built (<root epic>)` instead. Otherwise `○ Nothing ready to run`, in grey, with a line for each of what holds the tickets left: `2 questions wait for your answer: orchestra answer`, `3 tickets wait on other tickets: bd blocked`, `1 ticket waits for its subtickets to merge`, `2 tickets wait for their blockers to merge`, `1 follow-up waits for the ticket it was filed from to merge`, `1 in progress · 1 deferred`, `1 closed but not merged: bd list --label unmerged`. A run scoped with `--ticket` says `○ Nothing under <id> is ready to run`, then each of its tickets not done with why, as `SCOPE_OPEN` gives it. With `--plain`, or when output isn't a terminal, the same lines are printed plain, after the time. With nothing ready but tickets waiting on questions, and orchestra in a pane, the dashboard opens all the same, with the answer talk beside it, and the run takes up each ticket an answer frees (see [Nothing to run](running.md#nothing-to-run)).

Everything is also appended to `.orchestra/orchestra.log` in plain text, so `tail -f` works too. When output isn't a terminal, input isn't one either (the dashboard's keys come in there) or `TERM=dumb`, or with `--plain`, it prints those log lines instead of the live view. Lines that come in as the dashboard closes, such as a triage verdict or a warning, are printed below the run's summary. Scripts and agents should read the [event stream](events.md#event-stream) instead, whose records don't change with the log's wording.

On macOS, a notification tells you each outcome that matters, in a few words, titled with the project's folder name (`jswallet`): which ticket was closed, set aside or needs your answer, or that the run stopped or finished.

| When | It says |
|---|---|
| a ticket is merged | `Closed jswallet-12 · Add a --json flag to list` |
| a ticket is deferred, or left for review (`CHECKS_FAILED`, `MERGE_CONFLICT`, closed without a commit, …) | `Set aside jswallet-12 · checks failed` |
| a ticket waits on a question for you | `jswallet-12 needs your answer · Which name should the flag have?` |
| a worker reaches the account's usage limit, and the run holds for it (once per hold) | `Usage limit reached · resets 14:05`, or `Usage limit reached · the run waits` when Claude Code didn't say when |
| the run stops | `Stopped: PAUSED on jswallet-12`, or `Stopped: INTERRUPTED` when no ticket stopped it (`Stopped: USAGE_LIMIT on jswallet-12` after waiting out `usage_limit_wait`) |
| the run finishes | `Finished the run · 12 tickets closed · 2 set aside`, counting the tickets still set aside as it ends: not those reopened or merged since |

A title or reason longer than 60 characters is cut with `…`; the log has the rest. Holds other than the usage limit's, probes, the warnings that set no ticket aside (`LIKELY_CONFLICT`, `LONG_RUNNING`, …), triage and the run report don't notify: they are in the log, the event stream and the dashboard. Nor does a run with nothing to run. `NOTIFY=0`, or `--notify=false`, turns notifications off.

## The answer talk

A worker that needs your decision files a question, an issue labelled `human` that blocks the ticket waiting on it, with 2 to 4 options, one of them recommended ([Ask, don't wait](workers.md#worker-prompt)); the ticket shows as **? for you** and counts under **Needs you**. You answer it in the answer talk: a Claude Code session in the main checkout, in a pane split off orchestra's, given the open questions in the run's scope in `.orchestra/run/questions.md`, each with its options, the recommended one first, the tickets it blocks and those it was asked from. It asks each with Claude Code's options prompt, the recommended option first and marked and an Other line for your own words, talks it through when you ask, writes the decision into each blocked ticket's design and records your answer with `bd human respond`, which closes the question. It reads the repository and runs `bd` and read-only `git`, and edits no file. The run goes on meanwhile, and takes up a ticket an answer frees as it does whoever answered it (`ANSWERED`).

The dashboard opens the talk by itself as soon as a question waits, without taking the keyboard focus: the hint reads `a question for you · a to answer` (`2 questions for you · a to answer`), and **a** goes to the talk's pane. A question that comes to wait while it is open is queued into it (`.orchestra/run/questions-<n>.md`), given to claude once its turn is over: one pane, never one per question. With every question answered, the hint reads `answering in the pane on the right` until claude's turn is over, and orchestra closes the pane; `/exit` in the session leaves the rest open, for **a** to open the talk on again. `"questions": "key"` in `settings.json` opens it only on **a**, with the focus (`a to answer 2 questions`); `"auto"` is the default. Should the talk fail, why shows on a line above the hint in yellow, `! the answer talk: …`, and in the log, as when bd has none of its questions open any more (`the answer talk: bd has no question open in the run's scope: …`). The log says each time it opens (`the answer talk opens on the a key`, or `by itself, unfocused`) and how it ended (`the answer talk answered <question>`), even once the dashboard has closed, as when **a** comes as the run ends: orchestra then waits for a talk still open. A run with [nothing ready but questions](running.md#nothing-to-run) opens it whatever `"questions"` says.

The talk opens beside the dashboard only where orchestra runs in a pane (of Herdr, tmux or cmux): elsewhere, with `--plain`, or once the run is over, `orchestra answer` runs the same talk on the terminal it is typed in, where claude takes the terminal and `/exit` comes back. `orchestra answer --ticket <id>` asks only the questions blocking the ticket or one under it, or under it themselves, and `orchestra answer <question>` the one named; it ends saying which it answered, whose tickets come back to the run going on or to the next, and which are still open. Exit codes: 0 talked through (or none open), 2 it couldn't start, 130 stopped. On the command line alone, `bd human respond <question> --response "…"` answers a question too, as the log's `ASKED` line says: `ASKED: <id> waits on your answer to <question> (…); answer with: the dashboard's a key, orchestra answer <question> or bd human respond <question>; …`.
